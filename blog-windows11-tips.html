5 Windows 11 Tips Every Business User Should Know

By Mohammad Khurshed Alam·KhurshedTech IT Solutions·5 min read

Most of the support tickets we see are not caused by broken hardware — they are caused by small Windows settings nobody ever showed the user. Here are five that consistently save time for the businesses we support in Kuwait and remotely.

1. Use Snap Layouts to Stop Fighting With Windows

Hover over the maximize button (or press Windows + Z) and Windows 11 shows a grid of layout options — side-by-side, quarters, or a large window with two smaller ones. For anyone working with email, a spreadsheet and a browser at the same time, this alone can save several minutes a day of manual window resizing.

2. Control Update Timing Instead of Getting Surprised by It

Go to Settings → Windows Update → Advanced options → Active hours and set your actual working hours. Windows will avoid restarting for updates during that window. Combine this with "Pause updates" before an important presentation so a forced restart never happens at the worst possible moment.

3. Turn On Focus Assist During Deep Work

Settings → System → Focus assist lets you silence notifications automatically during set hours or when you're presenting or gaming (i.e., screen-sharing). For staff doing focused work like reporting or design, this cuts down on the constant ping of Teams and Outlook notifications pulling attention away.

4. Clean Up Storage Automatically With Storage Sense

Settings → System → Storage → Storage Sense automatically clears temporary files and old Recycle Bin items on a schedule you choose. On older or smaller-SSD business laptops, this alone often buys back several gigabytes without any manual cleanup — and prevents the "disk full" tickets that usually land on our desk.

5. Use Clipboard History for Repetitive Copy-Paste Work

Press Windows + V to open clipboard history — Windows remembers your last several copied items, not just the most recent one. For anyone doing repetitive data entry, filling forms, or pulling quotes from multiple documents, this removes a huge amount of back-and-forth switching between windows.

None of these require IT involvement to turn on — but if your team is on a mix of Windows versions, editions, or managed devices where some of these settings are locked down by policy, that's exactly the kind of thing a managed IT support plan keeps consistent across your whole office.
