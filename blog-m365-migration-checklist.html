Microsoft 365 Migration Checklist for Small Businesses

By Mohammad Khurshed Alam·KhurshedTech IT Solutions·6 min read

Moving mailboxes and files to Microsoft 365 is routine work for us, but for a business doing it for the first time, it can feel risky — nobody wants to lose email or have staff locked out on a Monday morning. Here's the checklist we actually follow.

1. Audit What You Actually Have

Before touching anything, list every mailbox, shared mailbox, distribution list, and where files currently live (on-premises server, another cloud provider, or local PCs). Note mailbox sizes — large, old mailboxes take longer to migrate and need to be scheduled accordingly.

2. Verify Domain Ownership and Plan DNS Changes

Microsoft 365 needs to verify you own your email domain, and mail flow depends on MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records. Plan the DNS cutover window in advance with whoever manages your domain — this is the single most common cause of "email stopped working" during a migration when it's rushed.

3. Choose a Migration Method That Matches Your Source

Small mailbox counts from another cloud provider (like Google Workspace) can usually use a direct IMAP or cutover migration. On-premises Exchange environments typically need a staged or hybrid migration. Choosing the wrong method for your source system is where most delays and data-loss risk come from.

4. Migrate a Test Batch First

Never migrate everyone at once. Move 1–2 non-critical mailboxes first, confirm mail flow, calendar items, and folder structure all came across correctly, then proceed with the rest in scheduled batches — usually outside business hours.

5. Set Up Multi-Factor Authentication Before Go-Live

Every account should have MFA enabled before it becomes the primary mailbox — not after. Migrating first and "securing it later" is how compromised accounts happen in the first week.

6. Train Staff on What's Changing

A short walkthrough of Outlook, Teams, OneDrive and SharePoint — even 20 minutes — prevents the majority of "where did my files go" tickets in the first week after go-live.

7. Keep the Old System Live (Briefly) as a Safety Net

Don't decommission the old mail system or file server immediately. Keep it accessible (read-only if needed) for a few weeks in case anything was missed in the migration — then decommission once everyone has confirmed everything is accounted for.
