Cybersecurity Basics Every Small Business Needs

By Mohammad Khurshed Alam·KhurshedTech IT Solutions·5 min read

Most small-business breaches we see are not sophisticated attacks — they're the result of a handful of missing basics. You don't need an enterprise security budget to close most of the gap. Here's where to start.

1. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on Every Account

This is the single highest-impact control available. If a password is stolen or guessed, MFA stops the attacker from getting in anyway. Enable it on email, Microsoft 365, banking portals and any system holding customer or financial data — no exceptions, including for the owner's own account.

2. Keep Windows and Software Actually Updated

Unpatched software is one of the most common entry points for ransomware. Set devices to install updates automatically, and don't let laptops sit unpatched for months because "it's working fine." A managed patching schedule closes this gap without disrupting anyone's day.

3. A Real Backup That's Actually Tested

A backup that has never been tested is not a backup — it's a guess. Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of your data, on two different types of storage, with one copy off-site or in the cloud. Then actually restore a test file periodically to confirm it works.

4. Email Security and Staff Awareness

Phishing remains the most common way attackers get in. Enable spam and phishing filtering on your email platform, and run a short, plain-English staff briefing on what a suspicious email looks like — unexpected invoice attachments, urgent "wire transfer" requests, and links that don't match the sender's real domain.

5. Limit Who Has Admin Access

Most staff never need administrator rights on their own computer, and very few people in the business need admin access to your Microsoft 365 tenant or servers. Fewer admin accounts means a stolen password does far less damage.

6. Endpoint Protection on Every Device

Modern endpoint protection (beyond basic antivirus) actively watches for suspicious behavior, not just known virus signatures. Every laptop, desktop and server that touches your business data should have it — including personal devices used for work email, if that's allowed at your business.

None of this requires a security team. It requires picking a partner who checks these basics as routine maintenance, not as a one-time project.
